American Wisteria (*Wisteria frutescens*) is a deciduous, twining vine native to the southeastern United States. It grows 15–30 feet, with glossy green leaves and produces dense, cascading clusters of fragrant, bluish-purple flowers in late spring to early summer. Unlike invasive Asian wisterias, it is less aggressive and blooms on new growth. This plant thrives in full sun to partial shade and prefers moist, well-drained soils. It attracts pollinators like bees and butterflies, enhancing garden biodiversity. American Wisteria is commonly used in arbors, trellises, and fences, providing a beautiful and controlled ornamental addition to landscapes without overwhelming native vegetation.
